# Vertex-model \(\sigma_{xx}\) and \(\sigma_{yy}\)

Generated by Cursor. 2026-09-20. Derivation and finite-difference test for `VertexQuadraticEnergy::getSigmaXX` and `getSigmaYY`. Same chain rule as `getSigmaXY`.

## Derivation

The quadratic vertex energy is

\[
E=\sum_i E_i,\qquad
E_i=K_A(A_i-A_{0,i})^2+K_P(P_i-P_{0,i})^2.
\]

Stress is the area-normalized derivative of \(E\) under an affine box strain, evaluated at zero strain. \(A_{\mathrm{box}}=L_x L_y\) is the **unstrained** box area (same convention as `getSigmaXY`).

| Component | Deformation \(F\) | Vertex map | \(\partial v/\partial\varepsilon\) |
|-----------|-------------------|------------|--------------------------------------|
| \(\sigma_{xy}\) | \(\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) | \((x,y)\to(x+\gamma y,y)\) | \((y_{\mathrm{rel}},0)\) |
| \(\sigma_{xx}\) | \(\begin{pmatrix}1+\varepsilon_x&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) | \((x,y)\to((1+\varepsilon_x)x,y)\) | \((x_{\mathrm{rel}},0)\) |
| \(\sigma_{yy}\) | \(\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1+\varepsilon_y\end{pmatrix}\) | \((x,y)\to(x,(1+\varepsilon_y)y)\) | \((0,y_{\mathrm{rel}})\) |

Uniaxial strain changes the box area at finite \(\varepsilon\). The analytic derivative is still taken at \(\varepsilon=0\), so the \(1/A_{\mathrm{box}}\) factor uses the unstrained area.

In the vertex model the degrees of freedom are the vertex positions \(\{v_j\}\):

\[
\frac{\partial E}{\partial\varepsilon}
=\sum_j \frac{\partial E}{\partial v_j}\cdot\frac{\partial v_j}{\partial\varepsilon}.
\]

Both factors are already in the force routine. `computeForceSetVertexModel` (in `src/utility/functions.h`) writes force sets

\[
F=-\frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{cell}}}{\partial v}
\]

into `vertexForceSets`. `voroCur` stores the same cell-relative vertex coordinates used to compute that force. Affine strain acts the same on relative coordinates (the cell centroid moves with the vertices), so \(x_{\mathrm{rel}}\) and \(y_{\mathrm{rel}}\) are `voroCur.x` and `voroCur.y`.

The minus sign matches \(F=-\nabla E\). Summing over the \(3 N_{\mathrm{vertices}}\) cell–vertex force sets gives

\[
\sigma_{xx}
=-\frac{1}{A_{\mathrm{box}}}\sum_{\text{force sets}} F_x\, x_{\mathrm{rel}},
\qquad
\sigma_{yy}
=-\frac{1}{A_{\mathrm{box}}}\sum_{\text{force sets}} F_y\, y_{\mathrm{rel}}.
\]

Those sums are `VertexQuadraticEnergy::getSigmaXX()` and `getSigmaYY()`. This is Voronoi’s uniaxial \(\varepsilon_x\) / \(\varepsilon_y\) (not area-preserving pure shear `getdEdepsilon`). Vertices are independent DOFs, so there is no circumcenter `dHdex` / `dHdey`.

## Numerical test

Independent random Voronoi initializations, \(N=4096\) cells, \(A_0=1\), \(K_A=K_P=1\), \(p_0=3.7,3.8,3.9\), \(10\) snapshots each. No equilibration. Topology is held fixed: vertices and the box are strained affinely, with no T1s.

- XX driver `vertexSigmaXX_pScan.cpp`: \(x\to(1+\varepsilon)x\), \(L_x\to(1+\varepsilon)L_x\).
- YY driver `vertexSigmaYY_pScan.cpp`: \(y\to(1+\varepsilon)y\), \(L_y\to(1+\varepsilon)L_y\).

The finite-difference check is the central difference of the total energy,

\[
\sigma^{\mathrm{num}}
=\frac{E(+\delta\varepsilon)-E(-\delta\varepsilon)}{2\,\delta\varepsilon\, A_{\mathrm{box}}},
\]

compared to the analytic value above. The plotted quantity is the mean absolute error over the snapshots,

\[
\langle|\sigma^{\mathrm{ana}}-\sigma^{\mathrm{num}}|\rangle.
\]

Run the two components separately (two binaries, two CSVs).

## Error versus \(\delta\varepsilon\)

A central difference of a smooth \(E(\varepsilon)\) has a Taylor remainder \(\propto(\delta\varepsilon)^2\). Each energy evaluation also carries a small floating-point error \(\delta E\). Dividing the noisy difference by \(2\delta\varepsilon A_{\mathrm{box}}\) produces a second term that grows as \(1/\delta\varepsilon\):

\[
\langle|\Delta\sigma|\rangle
\sim C\,(\delta\varepsilon)^2 + \frac{\delta E}{2\,\delta\varepsilon\, A_{\mathrm{box}}}.
\]

The first term dominates at large \(\delta\varepsilon\) (truncation). The second dominates at small \(\delta\varepsilon\) (roundoff). The sum is a U-shape; the analytic derivative is confirmed by the left branch dropping \(\sim 100\times\) per decade of \(\delta\varepsilon\). Uniaxial strain changes cell area at first order, so the left-branch prefactor \(C\) can be larger than for \(\sigma_{xy}\).

## How to run

From the repo root, after adding `vertexSigmaXX_pScan` and `vertexSigmaYY_pScan` to the local `CMakeLists.txt` and rebuilding:

```
localTest/executable/vertexSigmaXX_pScan.out -g -1 -n 4096 -c 10
localTest/executable/vertexSigmaYY_pScan.out -g -1 -n 4096 -c 10
python3 localTest/plot_vertexSigmaXXYY_pScan.py
```

CSV: `localTest/data/vertexSigmaXX_pScan.csv`, `localTest/data/vertexSigmaYY_pScan.csv`.

## Result (2026-09-20)

Both components show the same U-shape as \(\sigma_{xy}\). For \(p_0=3.8\), \(N=4096\), 10 snapshots:

| \(\delta\varepsilon\) | mean \(|\Delta\sigma_{xx}|\) | mean \(|\Delta\sigma_{yy}|\) |
|------------------------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| \(10^{-2}\) | \(9.51\times10^{-5}\) | \(9.48\times10^{-5}\) |
| \(10^{-3}\) | \(9.51\times10^{-7}\) | \(9.48\times10^{-7}\) |
| \(10^{-4}\) | \(9.49\times10^{-9}\) | \(9.48\times10^{-9}\) |
| \(10^{-5}\) | \(2.16\times10^{-10}\) | \(2.53\times10^{-10}\) |
| \(10^{-8}\) | \(1.97\times10^{-7}\) | \(2.10\times10^{-7}\) |

The left branch drops \(\sim 100\times\) per decade. Truncation is larger than for \(\sigma_{xy}\) because uniaxial strain changes cell area at first order. Figures: `localTest/data/vertexSigmaXX_pScan_abserr.png`, `vertexSigmaYY_pScan_abserr.png`.
